PROPOSED HARDWARE POLICY — NOT ACCEPTED
The current production checkpoint identity includes GPU UUID/name/driver and exact
Python/package/build/float-policy records. UUID identifies a device, not merely a
GPU model. A replacement Colab VM has no promise of the same device. Thus the current
policy supports process recovery on retained hardware but is not a dependable plan
for continuation after arbitrary Colab runtime loss. No guard was weakened.

Proposed alternative for explicit prospective approval: preserve old and new UUIDs
as provenance; define an approved compatibility class using GPU model/architecture,
compute capability, driver, CUDA/cuDNN, TensorFlow/Python/wheel hashes, determinism,
float32 and kernel/device configuration. Require exact within-device A/B/C and
cross-device checkpoint replay against independently retained uninterrupted reference
traces before accepting that class. Mismatch rejects exact continuation; no tolerance
relaxation, CPU fallback or weights-only loading. Matching names alone is insufficient.
Finite bounded replay does not prove all50 epochs. The reviewer must decide scope of
additional full-data/budget evidence and whether a weaker statistical reproducibility
claim would constitute a further scientific amendment. No such claim is approved.
If same-device recovery is required, consider a separately authorized persistent
hardware allocation. This task provisions nothing and assumes no paid resources.

Route B preserves the intended normal sampling distribution but changes the random
stream from original K5. K5/K7 comparisons must disclose RNG and environment changes;
do not attribute differences to K alone. The scientific amendment remains pending.
